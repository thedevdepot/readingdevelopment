Reading Resources

Welcome to your LaunchPad toolkit! Whether you are looking for your next favorite book, need free audiobooks, or want tools to help adjust how text looks on your screen, we have compiled the best resources right here.

Free Digital Libraries and Tools

These platforms offer thousands of free books, matching the resource links found in our application footer.

Gateway (Bible Gateway)

  • What it is: A searchable online Bible in hundreds of versions and languages.
  • Best for: Finding specific verses, comparing translations, or using audio tools to listen to scripture read aloud.

Project Gutenberg

  • What it is: A library of over 70,000 free eBooks, focusing on older literary classics where the copyright has expired.
  • Best for: Finding high school ELA classics (like Shakespeare, Jane Austen, or Mark Twain) that you can download or read directly in your browser.

Internet Archive

  • What it is: A massive digital library offering millions of free books, movies, software, and music.
  • Best for: Accessing historical texts, out-of-print books, and millions of accessible public domain materials.

The Poetry Foundation

  • What it is: An extensive collection of classic and contemporary poems, poet biographies, and audio recordings.
  • Best for: Readers looking for shorter text blocks, rhythm, or creative language practice.

Libby (by OverDrive)

  • What it is: An app that connects directly to your local public library card.
  • Best for: Borrowing modern ebooks, graphic novels, and audiobooks on your phone, tablet, or computer entirely for free.

Expanded Free and Safe Reading Platforms

To give you even more options, here are additional educator-approved, student-safe platforms that provide free reading materials without sketchy pop-ups, hidden costs, or mature content.

CommonLit

  • What it is: A free, highly vetted digital library designed specifically for students in grades 3-12.
  • Best for: Short stories, poems, and news articles categorized exactly by reading grade level. It includes a built-in "Read Aloud" tool and translation support.

Unite for Literacy

  • What it is: A beautifully simple digital library filled with hundreds of colorful, real-picture non-fiction books.
  • Best for: Emerging readers (Grades 1-3) or students practicing foundational vocabulary. Every book can be narrated aloud in dozens of languages.

Digital Public Library of America (DPLA)

  • What it is: A massive gateway that brings together millions of safe, historical photos, books, and articles from libraries across America.
  • Best for: History buffs and research projects. It features curated Primary Source Sets that make exploring history visually engaging.

Storynory

  • What it is: A safe, beautifully produced audio resource that has been releasing free audiobooks for kids since 2005.
  • Best for: Listening to myths, fairy tales, classic short stories, and original audio tales. Perfect for training listening comprehension skills.

Specialized Reading Extensions

If standard fonts or layouts make reading feel like a chore, try adding these free tools to your web browser to customize your experience.

  • OpenDyslexic Font Extension: A browser add-on that changes the text on every website to a font designed specifically to increase readability for readers with dyslexia.
  • NaturalReader (Text-to-Speech): A free extension that can read any web page, PDF, or uploaded document aloud in natural-sounding voices so you can listen along while you read.
  • BeeLine Reader: A tool that applies a subtle color gradient to text, wrapping your eyes smoothly from the end of one line to the beginning of the next. It helps with tracking text for readers with ADHD or dyslexia.

Quick Tips for Choosing a Book

Not sure what to read next? Use the Five-Finger Rule to see if a text is in your proximal learning zone:

  1. Open the book to any middle page and start reading.
  2. Put up one finger for every word you do not know or cannot easily pronounce.
  3. Count your fingers at the end of the page: 0-1 fingers means too easy (great for a quick, relaxing read).
  4. 2-3 fingers means just right. This is your target learning zone.
  5. 4 fingers means challenging and best with a teacher, parent, or support tool.
  6. 5 or more fingers means too hard for right now, so save it for later.
